Add Hysteresis to a Noisy Threshold (Schmitt Trigger)

Hint

A nudge in the right direction. The full answer is on the worked-solution page, so nothing below gives it away.

What you are being asked for

Two thresholds instead of one, so that turning ON and turning OFF happen at different places. Between them is a dead zone where nothing you do should change the state, no matter which way the reading is drifting.

Two rules, and an "otherwise"

  • Currently OFF, and the reading reaches high or above: turn ON.
  • Currently ON, and the reading falls to low or below: turn OFF.
  • Anything else: stay exactly where you were.

Notice which threshold each rule checks against: turning ON only ever looks at high; turning OFF only ever looks at low. Neither rule looks at the other's threshold.

The trap the problem is built around

A single midpoint threshold (reading >= (low + high) / 2, say) will pass a test where the signal moves cleanly from below low to above high and back -- there is nothing in a clean crossing to tell single-threshold and two-threshold logic apart. It falls over the moment the signal spends several readings wandering inside the dead zone: a fixed test does exactly that, holding every reading strictly between low and high, and checking the state never moves once. A single-threshold implementation will flip state as the noisy readings cross its one line back and forth; the two-threshold version, correctly implemented, does not react to any of them.

Watch out, beyond the dead zone

  • This is not the same technique as switch debouncing. Debouncing waits for several consecutive samples to agree before trusting a change -- it is about noise in time. Hysteresis reacts to a single reading immediately, as long as that reading has cleared a wide enough gap in value. Different noise, different fix.

Check yourself before you press Go

  • Starting OFF with low = 20, high = 80: readings 50, 79 keep it OFF; 80 turns it ON.
  • Once ON, a reading of 21 keeps it ON; only 20 or below turns it OFF.
