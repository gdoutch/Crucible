Decode a Multiplexed CAN Signal Group

Hint

A nudge in the right direction. The full answer is on the worked-solution page, so nothing below gives it away.

Read the multiplexor in isolation first

uint8_t mux = (uint8_t)(frame[0] & 0x0FU);

Do this before anything else, and dispatch on mux, not on frame[0]. Every other decision in this function depends on getting this one masked value right first.

Length before bytes

Once you know which multiplexor you have, you know exactly how many bytes of frame that case needs. Compare length against that requirement -- and reject if it falls short -- before indexing any byte that case would read. There is no partial decode to fall back to.

Little-endian 16-bit reassembly, one more time

uint32_t word = (uint32_t)frame[i] | ((uint32_t)frame[i + 1] << 8);

Byte i is the low half, byte i + 1 the high half -- same as the earlier CAN unpacking exercise, applied here to the bytes 1-2 pair for mux 0's first field.

Sign-extending a 12-bit field out of a 16-bit word

Mask to the 12 bits that matter (word & 0x0FFFU) before asking anything about sign -- the top 4 bits of the 16-bit word are not part of the value at all, not even as extra precision. Then check bit 11 of the *masked* 12-bit result (0x0800), not bit 15 of the original word: if it is set, the value is negative, and the standard trick for widening it is to subtract 1 << 12 (that is, 4096) from the unsigned 12-bit value once it is stored in a wide-enough signed type.

The 8-bit case is the one you already know

An 8-bit two's-complement field sign-extends the same way a `signed char` does going into a wider `int` -- casting through `int8_t` and back out to `int32_t` gets the compiler to do exactly that conversion for you, correctly, without hand-rolling the subtract-256 arithmetic.
