Fix the Analogue Output Correction

Hint

A nudge in the right direction. The full answer is on the worked-solution page, so nothing below gives it away.

What you are being asked for

Not to write a linear correction from scratch — the one in the editor is a perfectly normal shape for the job: multiply by a gain, subtract an offset, divide back down, clamp to range. You are being asked why a function built out of four ordinary, individually-correct-looking steps produces the opposite of the right answer at one end of its input range.

Start from the number, not the code

Put raw = 0 through the four steps by hand, in order, and write down what dw actually holds after each one. Not "it goes negative" — dw cannot go negative, and that fact is the whole problem. Write down the actual value.

StepExpressionWhat dw holds after
1dw *= AO_GAIN;still 0
2dw -= AO_OFFSET;?
3dw /= AO_SCALE;?
4if (dw > AO_FULL_SCALE) dw = AO_FULL_SCALE;?

Fill in row 2 first. dw is declared uint32_t. What does an unsigned type do with a subtraction it cannot represent?

It does not clamp to 0. It does not saturate. It does not raise anything. C defines exactly what happens, and the definition has nothing to do with "negative" — unsigned types don't have a negative side to fall back to.

Where to look

The final if is correct C, doing exactly what its one line says: if this got too big, clamp it down. Read row 2 again with that in mind. Is the value sitting in dw by the time it reaches that line actually too big — or does it only look that way to a comparison that has no way to tell "too big" apart from "wrapped past zero"?

That is the whole defect. Nothing after step 2 is wrong; everything after step 2 is correctly propagating what step 2 handed it.

Why 0..3 and not just 0

The bug report only mentions a stopped motor, i.e. raw = 0. The test suite pushes on 1, 2 and 3 as well, and they fail exactly the same way. Work out the condition under which step 2 is safe — in terms of raw, AO_GAIN and AO_OFFSET — and the four-count width of the band stops being a mystery and becomes arithmetic you can check.

Still stuck? A bigger nudge

Add this as the first line inside the function, temporarily:

printf("raw=%u after-mul=%lu\n", raw, (unsigned long)(raw * (uint32_t)AO_GAIN));

(#include <stdio.h> at the top; take it back out before you submit, since the tests compare stdout exactly.) Run it for raw = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and compare after-mul against AO_OFFSET (3000) each time. One of those five is the first where after-mul is actually bigger than AO_OFFSET — that is the boundary the tests are drawing your attention to, and it's a comparison you can make before the subtraction runs at all.

When you think you have it

  • The fix touches step 2 only. AO_GAIN, AO_SCALE, AO_FULL_SCALE and the final clamp are all correct as written — if your change touches any of those, you've gone further than the defect goes.
  • Check 4095 and 4200 still both come out at 4095. The clamp has a real job to do near the top of the range, and your fix should not stop it doing it.
  • Check 500 still comes out at 492. A fix that only special-cases small inputs, rather than making the subtraction safe in general, is a second bug wearing the first one's clothes.
